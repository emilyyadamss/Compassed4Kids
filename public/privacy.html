Privacy policy

Last updated 4 September 2026

This app holds a record of a child's homework, and that record belongs to the child and to their parent. It is not sold, rented, brokered, or handed to advertisers. This page says what is collected, where it is kept, who can reach it, and how to get rid of it.

The short version

  • Your data is never sold. Not to advertisers, not to data brokers, not to schools or tutoring companies. That is not a current policy that could quietly change; it is what this app is.
  • No ads, no trackers, no analytics. There is no advertising SDK, no tracking pixel, and no third-party analytics anywhere in the app.
  • Only your family can see your family. Every row is tied to your account and enforced by the database itself, not merely by the screens you are shown.
  • Everything is encrypted in transit and at rest, in Supabase's managed Postgres.
  • You can export all of it, or delete all of it, from Settings, at any time, without asking anyone.

What this covers

The Compassed 4 Kids web app, and the small server function behind its quiz feature. It is addressed to the parent who holds the account, because in this app the parent is the account: a child does not sign up, does not have a login, and does not have a password.

What is collected

Your account

An email address and a password — or an email address alone, if you use the emailed sign-in link. Sign-in is handled by Supabase Auth. Your password is hashed by Supabase and is never stored, logged, or visible in plain text to anyone, us included.

What you set up for your kids

  • Each kid: the name you type, an optional grade, and the avatar and color you pick. A first name is enough. The app never asks for a surname, a birthday, an address, a school, a photograph, or any way of contacting a child, and it is better if you do not type one in.
  • Each activity: its name, icon, the days it is expected on, how it is counted, any description you write, and any quiz questions you write yourself.
  • Each check-off: the date, the amount recorded, whatever was typed in the note box about what was done, and the quiz score if that activity was quizzed.
  • Your settings: theme, notification preference, the hour after which a day counts as behind, whether quizzes are on, and the four-digit parent PIN if you set one.

On your device

Your browser keeps your Supabase sign-in session, so you are not asked to log in on every visit, and one small value recording whether this device was last left in kid mode — which is what lets the kitchen tablet reopen into your child's screen rather than your dashboard. If you switch on desktop notifications, that permission is granted to your browser, by you, and can be taken back there.

What is not collected

  • No advertising or marketing identifiers, and no profile of your family for anyone to target.
  • No analytics, session recording, heatmaps, or behavioural tracking of any kind.
  • No location, contacts, camera, microphone, or files from your device.
  • No payment details — the app does not take payments.
  • No accounts, logins, or credentials belonging to a child.

Where it is kept, and how it is kept safe

Your family's data lives in a Supabase project: managed Postgres, encrypted at rest, reached only over HTTPS. The app itself is a static site, also served over HTTPS.

Separation between families is enforced in the database rather than in the interface. Every table has row-level security switched on, and every policy says the same thing: a row is readable and writable only by the account that owns it. That holds for live updates too, so the check-off that appears on your phone the moment your child taps it can only ever be your own child's. A bug in a screen cannot show you another family's kids, because the database would not hand over their rows to begin with.

The parent PIN and kid mode are a lock on the interface, not a second account. They keep a child out of the grown-up dashboard on a shared tablet; they are not a defense against someone holding your email and password, which is the thing genuinely worth protecting. Use a password you use nowhere else.

Quizzes, and what reaches Claude

Quizzing is optional. It is off for every activity you have not switched it on for, and a single control in Settings turns the whole feature off.

When it is on and your child checks off a quizzed activity, they type a sentence or two about what they actually did. To turn that into questions, the server sends Anthropic's Claude API the activity name, the description you wrote for it, the amount logged, your child's first name and grade as you entered them, and what your child typed. Nothing else travels with it: not your email, not your account, not your other children, not your history.

Anthropic does not train its models on data submitted through the API. The answer key is encrypted before the quiz ever reaches the browser, so a child who opens the network tab finds ciphertext rather than answers. The server function keeps nothing — it has no database of its own, and a quiz exists only for the sitting it was written for.

If you would rather nothing at all left the app, turn quizzes off. Everything else — the lists, the check-offs, the streaks, the notifications — involves no third party beyond the database holding it.

Who else touches it

Only the services that make the app run. Each acts on our instructions; none of them is given your data for its own purposes.

Service What it does What it sees
Supabase Database, sign-in, live updates Your account and your family's data
Vercel Hosting, and the quiz server function Ordinary request logs; quiz requests in transit
Anthropic Writes quiz questions, only when quizzing is on Only the activity and note described above
Google Fonts Serves the typefaces Your IP address, as any web request would

Beyond these, data is disclosed only where the law genuinely requires it, and you will be told unless we are forbidden to tell you. If the app were ever transferred to someone else, you would hear about it first, and this policy would travel with the data.

Your data is not for sale

It is not sold, rented, licensed, or traded, and it is not shared for advertising, marketing, or anyone's idea of personalisation. That includes everything about your children: their names, their schedules, their grades, what they read, how they scored, and how often they finished their day. There is no exception to this and no partner arrangement sheltering behind a word like affiliate.

Children's privacy

The account belongs to a parent or guardian, who decides what goes into it. Children use the app through that account, in kid mode, and cannot create one of their own.

What is stored about a child is only what a parent typed and what the child checked off. The app asks for as little as it can work with — a first name, and optionally a grade — and that is never enriched, cross-referenced, or built into a profile. A parent can read, correct, export, or permanently delete everything about a child at any time, and deleting a kid takes their activities and their entire record with them.

How long it is kept, and how to be rid of it

  • While your account exists, your data stays as you left it. There is no separate archive, and no shadow copy of things you deleted.
  • Deleting a kid deletes their activities and every check-off with them, immediately, in the database.
  • Settings → Clear all data removes every kid, activity, and record on the account.
  • Deleting your account removes everything attached to it. Write to us and it will be done.
  • Settings → Export backup hands you the whole thing as a plain JSON file first, yours to keep.

Backups held by the infrastructure providers above roll off on their own schedule, typically within thirty days.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to see what is held about you, correct it, delete it, take a portable copy, or object to how it is handled. The app gives you most of these directly — export and deletion are buttons in Settings, not requests you have to file — and for anything else, write to us and it will be acted on. Nothing about the app gets worse for you for exercising any of it, and there is no sale to opt out of, because there is no sale.

Cookies

No advertising or tracking cookies are used. Your browser stores a Supabase sign-in session so that you stay signed in, plus the small kid-mode preference described above. Clearing this site's storage signs you out; your data is untouched and returns when you sign in again.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and anything that materially affects what happens to your family's data will be announced in the app before it takes effect.
